A SIMPLE EXPLANATION OF ML-KEM (POST-QUANTUM CRYPTOGRAPHY)
=============================================================

This document is written clearly so you can easily include it in your B.Tech project report or presentation. It explains the core concept of ML-KEM and how it replaces RSA.

### 1. What is ML-KEM?
ML-KEM stands for "Module-Lattice-Based Key Encapsulation Mechanism." It is the official algorithm standardized by the US Government (NIST) to protect data against future Quantum Computers. Before it was officially standardized as FIPS-203, it was known in the cryptography community as "CRYSTALS-Kyber".

### 2. Why is RSA Bad Against Quantum Computers?
RSA relies on the fact that standard computers are very slow at factoring enormous prime numbers. For a normal computer, breaking an RSA-2048 key could take millions of years. However, a powerful Quantum Computer running "Shor's Algorithm" can solve these prime number equations almost instantly, completely destroying RSA security. 

ML-KEM fixes this. Instead of prime numbers, ML-KEM hides its secrets inside a highly complex multi-dimensional mathematical grid called a "Lattice", using a problem called "Learning With Errors". Even the most powerful theoretical quantum computers cannot easily solve lattice problems.

### 3. How "Key Transport" (RSA) Works
To understand ML-KEM, it helps to review the old RSA way.
RSA is a "Key Transport" system. It works like a physical lockbox:
1. You generate a random secret password (the AES key).
2. You take the user's Public Key (the open lockbox) and put your AES key inside it.
3. You lock the box and send it across the internet or save it to a database.
4. The user takes their Private Key, unlocks the box, and gets the AES key out.

### 4. How "Key Encapsulation" (ML-KEM) Works
ML-KEM is completely different. You DO NOT create a secret key first and lock it inside a box. Instead, ML-KEM generates a mathematical puzzle.

**The Upload Process (Encapsulation):**
When a user uploads a file, the server feeds the user's ML-KEM Public Key into the algorithm. The math engine grinds and dynamically spits out two things simultaneously:
  1. A `Shared Secret` (This is your 256-bit AES Key).
  2. A `Ciphertext` (A mathematical puzzle that represents the secret).

You immediately use that `Shared Secret` to encrypt your file with AES. Then, you permanently destroy/delete the secret from your computer! All you do is save the `Ciphertext` puzzle piece in your database. 

**The Download Process (Decapsulation):**
When the user wants to download their file, they pull the weird `Ciphertext` puzzle from the database. 
They feed that `Ciphertext` AND their secret `Private Key` into the ML-KEM algorithm.
By combining the puzzle piece and the private key, the math engine acts like a magic trick and identically recreates the exact same `Shared Secret` that the server deleted earlier! 
The user then uses this magically recovered AES key to decrypt their file.

### Summary for your Project Report
In your project, the file itself is encrypted using standard AES-256. But the AES key is protected using Post-Quantum ML-KEM Encapsulation. Because the AES Key is mathematically derived and "encapsulated" into a Lattice matrix rather than wrapped in a standard prime-number equation, any data stored in your Secure Cloud Storage system is heavily protected against both modern hackers and future Quantum supercomputers.
